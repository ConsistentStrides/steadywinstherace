How to estimate your mile time:
Think about how fast you could run one mile if you gave it a real effort — not a jog, but not a sprint either. A sustained hard effort for about 4–10 minutes.

Quick guide by feel:
• Your easy jogging pace is probably 2–4 min/mile slower than your mile effort
• If you jog at 11–12 min/mile, your mile effort might be around 8–9 min
• If you jog at 9–10 min/mile, your mile effort might be around 7–8 min

When in doubt, go 15–20 seconds slower than you think — it's better to start conservative and adjust upward.

Quality sessions auto-placed 4 days before your long run. Rest day defaults to day after long run.
Quality sessions are structured workouts with a specific purpose — intervals, threshold runs, or pace work. They are the hard days that drive fitness improvements.

Q1 = Your long run (always included)
Q2 = Your primary workout (intervals or threshold)
Q3 = A second structured workout (threshold or pace run)

Easy (E) days fill the rest of the week to hit your mileage target. Most runners do well with 2–3 quality sessions and should never do them on back-to-back days without recovery between.
